About Me

Data. ML. Agents.

I'm Harry: engineer and scientist by training, business-minded by choice. I've spent 8+ years across Southeast Asia's most recognisable tech platforms: Shopee (ecommerce), Gojek (ride-hailing), and Vidio.com (OTT). I ran BI Engineering at Shopee Indonesia, where I owned the whole chain: data lake and warehouse foundations, dimensional models and standardised metrics, self-service datasets, governance, and the ML that sits on top of it.

I grew that team from 4 to 15+ engineers and set the direction they delivered against: self-service data mart adoption across the organisation rose from ~5% to ~60%, and governance coverage from 13% to 58%. I owned the design and delivery of the secure, automated pipelines behind ShopeePay's mandatory Bank Indonesia and OJK regulatory reporting. Great BI leadership isn't about dashboards. It's establishing the policies, standards and trusted metrics that let every team make autonomous decisions without waiting on an analyst.

The other half of the job is machine learning and AI. I've led end-to-end ML delivery spanning fraud detection, user profiling and computer vision, and saved ~$500K in manpower costs through LLM and agent-based automation that I built hands-on. I'm now completing a Master of Data Science at the University of Sydney to keep that edge sharp, and to lead the move from reporting to decision intelligence: ML that decides and agents that act.

Leadership

How I run a data function

Two beliefs run through everything I have built: that data should be democratised, and that AI is there to augment the people using it. I became a manager the slow way, by building the systems first, which is why I can still read the pull request and still know which corners are safe to cut.

01
Data belongs to everyone

I believe data should be democratised. Every team in an organisation should be able to answer its own questions without queuing behind an analyst. That is a delivery problem, not a slogan: it means taking data the whole way from raw to mart, at a quality people can rely on, and making it easy enough that they choose it over their own spreadsheets.

Self-service adoption ~5% to ~60%
02
Democratised data has to be trustworthy

Opening access to data that is wrong is worse than keeping it shut. Before I widen the door I want documented assets, standardised metrics, approved access paths and security aligned to ISO. Governance is not bureaucracy. It is what lets me say yes to more people, faster, and it is what makes AI possible later.

Governance coverage 13% to 58%
03
AI is there to augment people

Because I own the whole lifecycle, from raw ingestion through to the mart, I know that clean, trusted data is the engine AI runs on. That is where the two halves of my work meet. I put AI where it takes the manual work off people rather than replacing them, so teams spend their hours on the things only humans can do. Used properly it does not shrink what a team can attempt, it expands it.

~$500K of manual work automated
04
Stay close enough to build

I do not review architecture from a slide. I still write code, still deploy things, and that is what lets me size a project honestly, spot an estimate that is fiction, and keep the trust of engineers who know when a manager has drifted.

Shipping code in 2026, not just reviewing it
